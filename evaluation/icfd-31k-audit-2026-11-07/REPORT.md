# ICFD-31k adoption audit result

Completed: 2026-11-07. Decision: **do not use this pinned release as the primary benchmark for
evidence-grounded first warnings or as the sole workflow-versus-agent comparison.** It remains a
possible synthetic classification diagnostic under explicitly limited claims. No detector pilot is
recommended on it before deciding whether that limited role is useful.

This conclusion concerns the inspected release and code, not every version of ICFD-31k. Data were
not repaired, relabeled, translated or repartitioned. No model was trained or evaluated.

## Checks that passed

All 83 manifest shards downloaded: 64,617,715 bytes. All byte counts and SHA-256 hashes match both
the manifest and checksum list. All declared record totals match: 31,000 source conversations and
1,111,071 streaming chunks. Transfer took 33.62 seconds in this environment; that is a measurement
of this transfer, not an estimate for future runs.

No duplicate conversation UIDs, normalized exact full-conversation duplicates, cross-split UID/text
overlaps, or duplicate `(conversation_uid, chunk_timestamp)` groups were found. Stream-to-source split
membership is consistent. Cross-domain metadata names do not overlap main-domain names; this checks
release grouping, not independent semantic verification of the claimed category novelty.

The 1,111,038 chunks with checkable source timing exactly match their source prefixes. Another 33
have an uncheckable source time boundary. This supports the absence of future utterances in the
checkable released prefixes; it does not establish that their labels were judged without hindsight.

## Findings that limit adoption

### Source-label distribution differs sharply by split

| Split | Scam | Benign | Scam share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Train | 6,481 | 14,519 | 30.86% |
| Validation | 4,474 | 26 | 99.42% |
| Test | 4,477 | 23 | 99.49% |
| Cross-domain | 961 | 39 | 96.10% |

From those counts alone, always returning scam on the released static test would give 99.49%
accuracy and 99.74% positive-class F1 while falsely warning on every benign case. This is arithmetic,
not model inference. Only 23 benign test conversations support its false-alarm estimate. High
accuracy/F1 therefore cannot be treated as evidence of a useful warning system without further
analysis. These calculations must not be compared directly with scores from a different split.

### Chunk labels are generated by a keyword/time rule

The inspected [ChunkExpander source](upstream/src/generators/chunk_expander.py) searches any speaker's
text for substrings such as `verify`, `confirm`, `urgent`, `password` and `otp`. For a final scam
conversation, the first match supplies the boundary; absent a match, the fallback is 60% of total
conversation duration. Certain scenario case types force an infinite boundary. A final non-scam
conversation receives non-scam chunk labels.

**All 1,111,071 released chunk labels match that rule.** They are not simply copies of the final
conversation label, but they are also not independently established evidence-completing warnings.
The fallback depends on future total duration. A mention from the protected recipient or an ordinary
word match can set the boundary without a deceptive request. The rule's behavior is demonstrated on
invented public fixtures in [test_audit.py](test_audit.py).

There are 1,942 conversations with identical observed-prefix content receiving conflicting chunk
labels at different times. The native RoBERTa formatter supplies speaker/text, not the current clock,
so those identical textual inputs cannot support both labels deterministically. This is a limitation
of using those references to measure text-based warning timing, not evidence that future messages
were supplied to the model.

The source records also contain a different, sparse `chunk_level_analysis` annotation layer. At
51,674 comparable exact timestamps, it disagrees with released chunk labels 5,774 times (11.17%).
Neither layer was independently validated here. Do not treat them as interchangeable gold.

### Temporal/schema exceptions remain in the unmodified data

- Seven source conversations have missing or nonnumeric utterance timestamps; 19 have decreasing
  timestamps. Four source annotations lack a valid binary field; 21 have invalid timestamp types.
- The proposed strict input adapter rejects 563 chunks for invalid or unordered message timing.
- Six source conversations have no released chunks.
- For 27,763 conversations with chunks, the final utterance is not present in any chunk. The native
  three-second grid ends before the actual call end in these cases. A runner that adds the final
  utterance would use a different replay protocol and must disclose that change.

These are mechanical findings, not a full semantic label audit. No exception was silently removed
from the source population or accepted through a guessed conversion.

### Published baseline results are not directly reproduced by the release

Native loaders expect old per-batch JSON folders and recreate splits. The released data use fixed
JSONL/Zstandard and Parquet shards. A loader/split adapter is necessary; simply launching a training
script is not reproduction of the pinned release protocol.

Saved upstream numeric results were inspected without displaying case-level outputs:

- Static retest: 4,500 samples, F1 0.996335. A separate full-dataset result has 30,000 samples and
  F1 0.994017. The paper's rounded static F1/precision/recall match the latter aggregate; this numeric
  correspondence does not prove the original experiment's full provenance or an independent holdout.
- Saved streaming split: 22,145/4,745/4,746 sessions and 604,752/130,951/129,116 chunks. Those are not
  the current release's documented source/chunk split counts. No exact split-identity mapping was found.
- The Gemma loader consumes `cumulative_transcript` training chunks; its 5,000 saved samples are not
  established as 5,000 independent full conversations. Its scorer excludes failed outputs from primary
  metrics and maps non-YES predictions to NO. The preserved result reports zero execution errors.
- The streaming early-detection denominator uses majority chunk labels to decide which sessions count
  as scams. Applied to the release, it would omit 1,809 of the 4,476 test scam conversations that have
  chunks; one further test scam has no chunks. Its denominator is not all source-labeled scams.
- An ancillary Gemma setup script has a syntax error at line 19. It was not executed or repaired.

These findings prevent a claim of reproduced published baseline performance. No weights were downloaded,
no training scripts imported and no real detector predictions produced.

## Verification and reproducibility

The [13 public-fixture checks](tests.log) pass: known confusion matrix, undefined denominators,
failure versus no-warning, malformed inputs, exclusion of gold/metadata, future-message rejection,
native keyword/fallback behavior and malformed-source robustness. The audit contract reports undefined
ratios as null; upstream sklearn code uses zero in several such cases. That difference is explicit,
not a replacement score reported as native benchmark performance.

The final full-data scan completed in 69.97 seconds. Python 3.12.16, PyArrow 24.0.0, Zstandard 0.25.0
and SQLite 3.54.1 were used; [pixi.lock](pixi.lock) pins the environment. These are tooling checks,
not tests of a model's quality. No API inference, GPU job or LLM judge ran.

An initial diagnostic scan incorrectly discarded sources with malformed annotations, making some
chunks appear unlinked. A subsequent resume exposed another assumption about annotation fields.
The scanner was corrected to preserve every source, flag malformed fields separately and avoid
guessing conversions. Regression fixtures cover those paths. Earlier diagnostic logs/checkpoints are
retained but superseded by the signature in `data-audit.json`; their apparent orphan counts are not
upstream findings.

From the project root, after dependencies and the pinned files are available:

```sh
timeout 600s evaluation/icfd-31k-audit-2026-11-07/.pixi/envs/default/bin/python -I evaluation/icfd-31k-audit-2026-11-07/scan.py

```

The timeouts are operational limits. `retrieve.py --metadata-only` and `retrieve.py --download`
reconstruct pinned inputs with receipts; `review_training.py` reconstructs the training-only sample
without printing conversations. Checkpoints are keyed by source/manifest signatures; source changes
produce a new private checkpoint rather than combining incompatible runs.

## Consequence for this project

Hold ICFD-31k out of the proposed primary suite for evidence-grounded warnings. Do not spend a model
pilot merely to obtain a high score on this release. Static classification or agreement with the
native heuristic may still be studied as explicitly limited diagnostics; that is a separate choice.
English/Hinglish synthetic-call performance would not establish Vietnamese messaging performance.

Continue assessing existing candidates before investing further in new data. This audit does not
validate local synthetic-v1, demonstrate its novelty, or authorize new generation. No source dataset,
local benchmark, repository history, or teammate implementation was changed.
